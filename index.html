AI & Tech OpenAI countersues Apple over trade secret theft and alleged coercion of shared suppliers
🤖 AI & Tech • The Decoder
Matthias Bastian View the LinkedIn Profile of Matthias Bastian Nov 27, 2026
Short News

OpenAI is suing Apple for trade secret theft and unfair business practices. The ChatGPT maker filed the lawsuit Friday in a California court, the Financial Times reports. OpenAI accuses Apple of "coercing and threatening" suppliers that work with both companies and of using its market power to stifle competition.

The company also alleges that Apple encourages employees to store corporate data on personal devices, then turns that practice against staff who want to leave. According to the complaint, a former OpenAI employee passed along details about product plans and suppliers after returning to Apple.

The countersuit follows Apple's own lawsuit from the summer, which accused several former employees of leaking confidential information to OpenAI. The AI company had previously closed its $6.4 billion acquisition of former Apple chief designer Jony Ive's studio "io" and is preparing its own devices to rival Apple. Just two years ago, the two companies made a splashy announcement of a partnership to integrate ChatGPT into the iPhone.

Global Event Trump alleges ‘fake’ economic figures released before US midterms but doesn’t provide evidence
🌍 Major Global Event • The Guardian Economics

In a bitter Thanksgiving post, president seeks investigation after blue wave of Democrats unseated Republicans

Donald Trump has alleged without evidence that economic figures published by the US federal government before this month’s midterm elections were “fake” and demanded an investigation after voters roundly rejected his Republican party.

In a holiday message on social media tinged with bitterness, the US president wished a happy Thanksgiving “to even those who issued fake Economic Numbers, just prior to the Election, and corrected them yesterday, three weeks late, showing that the GDP was actually 4.3%, versus 3.4%”.

While revisions to official economic figures are commonplace, Trump has tried time and again to challenge trust in economic data that does not support his claims that a historic boom is under way in the US.

The US Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA), an agency within the US Department of Commerce, calculates gross domestic product, or GDP, numbers.

“Why wasn’t the correct number issued before the Election? I am asking for an immediate investigation of this situation,” Trump wrote on his Truth Social platform on Thursday. “This is not a small error, this is a massive, and purposeful, error”.

Trump has repeatedly claimed since taking office in January 2025 that an economic “golden age” is taking hold on his watch. Many Americans have been grappling with a higher cost of living, however – exacerbated by the economic fallout of his war on Iran – and backed Democrats in the midterms.

Trump continued his acerbic well-wishing for “a very Happy Thanksgiving to all of those people that have worked so hard to bring our Country to the Radical Far Left” as well as “those who want to redistribute Property, so that all of the hardworking people that have built up, over a lifetime, a certain Wealth, will now be forced to distribute it throughout the Country, even to those who refuse to work”.

And he revisited another ongoing beef with the news media over his controversial focus on White House renovations and decorations. “I want to commiserate with those reporters and citizens who don’t understand filth, dirt, lack of maintenance, and corruption, and are upset with me because I took time out of my very busy schedule to fix the 17 broken windows, numerous chipped and decaying columns, broken, dilapidated, and missing moldings, and lack of a proper plaster and paint job on the White House.”

The sarcastic intervention continued with a message of thanks to “the murderers, drug dealers, and criminals of all shapes, sizes, and colors” whom he claimed are “mostly gone now”.

“Happy Thanksgiving to them anyway, even though from faraway lands,” he concluded. “We miss you dearly, but you will never be back, and America will never allow what you have done to us to happen again!”

The Democratic senator Chuck Schumer, vying to be majority leader in the chamber following his party’s victories in this month’s election took a more conventional approach, wishing “everyone in New York and across America a joyous and restful Thanksgiving holiday with your friends and family”.

Hakeem Jeffries, whom Democrats have nominated to be the first Black speaker in the House of Representatives, also played it straight. “Happy Thanksgiving to everyone in the community and throughout our great country! Grateful for the civil servants on the job who are working through the stormy weather to keep everyone safe,” he posted on X. “May God bless you all.”

Indian Economy India’s milk production rises 2.4% to top 253 million tonnes in 2025-26
🇮🇳 Indian Economy • The Hindu BusinessLine

India’s milk production grew by 2.4 per cent to 253.81 million tonnes during 2025-26 over 247.87 million tonnes the previous year. The per capita availability of milk improved to 492 grams per day during 2025-26 against 485 grams per day the previous year, per the latest Basic Animal Husbandry Statistics 2026, released on Thursday.

However, the pace of growth in India’s milk production continued to slow down during the year. The growth rate in milk production slowed down to 2.4 per cent during 2025-26 from the previous year’s 3.58 per cent. In fact, the growth rate in milk output has more than halved in the past five years, even as the production volumes continue to rise.

“The sustained increase in milk production can be attributed to various initiatives and interventions undertaken by the Government to enhance dairy productivity, improve breeding infrastructure, strengthen animal health services, and support dairy farmers. These efforts have played a crucial role in meeting the growing demand for milk arising from the increasing human population and changing consumption patterns” the report said.

UP largest producer

Species wise, cattle accounted for 53.63 per cent of the total milk produced in the country. This comprised 32.98 per cent from the crossbred/exotic cattle and 20.65 per cent from the indigenous/non-descript cattle. Buffaloes accounted for 43.02 per cent of the total milk produced while goats contributed the remaining 3.35 per cent.

Uttar Pradesh was the largest milk producing state and accounted for 15.56 per cent of the country’s output during 2025-26. Rajasthan was the second largest producer with a share of 14.75 per cent followed by Madhya Pradesh with 8.93 per cent and Gujarat with a share of 7.86 per cent. Maharashtra with share of 6.69 per cent was the fifth largest milk producing state during 2025-26.

Tamil Nadu recorded the highest growth rate in milk production at 6.59 per cent over previous year, followed by Jharkhand at 5.57 per cent, Telangana at 4.82 per cent and Andhra at 3.67 per cent. Milk production growth rate in UP stood at 1.74 per cent, while in MP it was 0.36 per cent. In Rajasthan milk production grew 1.91 per cent. Gujarat saw a milk production growth rate of 3.39 per cent and Maharashtra 2.18 per cent.

FAO forecast

About nine states saw a milk production growth rate above the national average of 2.4 per cent. “The higher growth rates observed in these states indicate continued improvements in milk production and contribute significantly to the overall growth of the dairy sector in the country” the report said.

The Food and Agriculture Organisation (FAO) in its recent biannual global food outlook said the global milk production is forecast to expand further in 2026, albeit at a slower pace than in 2025. Global milk output is forecast to reach 1015.5 million tonnes in 2026, up 1.8 percent from the previous year, compared with 2.2 percent growth in 2025. India has been the largest milk producer accounting for around a fourth of the global output.

“Asia is expected to drive much of the expansion, led by India and Pakistan, where sustained domestic demand and productivity gains continue to support production growth, alongside an anticipated recovery in China,” FAO said.
